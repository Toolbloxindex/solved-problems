## Weakest step

The conclusions concern increasing ordinals, so fixed-index coefficient convergence would be insufficient. In the forward estimate the all-index majorant has a ratio bounded geometrically outside its two dominant terms. At the stated scale this ratio is (O(n^{-2})) for sufficiently small (c_1), while the binomial margin on the two circles is of order (1/j). The geometric-multiplicity law gives error (O((j+1)log(j+3)/n)) in the coefficient ratio. This is at most (1/(8j)) after reducing (c_1). The relative separation (1+1/j) between the two root scales is therefore large enough. The adjacent pivot scales separate by a factor tending to infinity uniformly on this range.

At the other endpoint, the absolute approximation error is of order (n^{-1}exp(C(j+1))) on a disk of radius ((j+1)^2). For (j ≤ c₂ log n + 1) and small enough (c_2), it is (o(1/(jH_{n+1}))). This is the necessary margin on the real test segments, stronger than the margin needed just to count zeros on the separating modulus circles. At (z = −j² + δ), isolating the (j)-th factor leaves a nonvanishing product. The sine product bounds its modulus below uniformly on (|δ| ≤ 5j/Hₙ₊₁). The marked product contributes (e_3/j), and (e₃/e₄ ∼ 4/Hₙ₊₁) uniformly with that factor omitted. Thus the partner is bracketed between offsets (-5j/H_{n+1}) and (-3j/H_{n+1}), whereas the pivot lies between (-j/H_{n+1}) and (j/H_{n+1}). The adjacent pivot squares are separated by (2j+1), so the two brackets and the next pivot remain correctly ordered. Rouché counts on consecutive separating circles give exactly one zero with multiplicity in each annulus. The real sign brackets therefore identify simple negative zeros. Taking reciprocals gives the asserted far-side gaps.

The extra factor (m) in the forward partner contributes (n+2+j) in the reversed coefficient and cancels its parity change. The ratio (a_{m-1}/a_m=(2m)(2m+1)/π²) absorbs this into the required factorial majorant. This checks the bound before restoring the shared denominator as well as the indispensable shift in the marked series.

## Verdict

Passed as a written proof of the two growing endpoint ranges. The computation was a check of the normalization and estimates. The proof is uniform in the stated ranges. The first gap from zero outside this proof has index (⌊c₁√(n/log n)⌋+1) when the proof's constants are fixed. The corresponding first gap outside this proof from the far end has index (⌊c₂ log n⌋+1). An all-rank comparison must cover the intervening gaps.
