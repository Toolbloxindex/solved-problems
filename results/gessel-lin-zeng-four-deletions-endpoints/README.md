# Growing interlacing ranges for four Jacobi–Stirling deletions

## In brief

Gessel, Lin and Zeng ask whether the descent polynomial obtained by summing equally over deletion sets has only real zeros. For four deletions, Ma and Wang propose proving the claim by fitting one zero of a new polynomial between each consecutive pair of zeros of a known polynomial. This work proves that the comparison holds in growing ranges at both ends for sufficiently large rank. Near zero, two adjacent coefficients control the signs. At the most negative end, reciprocating the variable brings the zeros near negative squares, where a finite product lets us locate both zero lists. The middle gaps and the full four-deletion conjecture remain open. [Gessel–Lin–Zeng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.0622v2) · [Ma–Wang, Remark 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03111v1#S4)

## What this settles

For some absolute constants \(c_1,c_2>0\) and every sufficiently large \(n=k-2\), the equal-weight partner in Ma–Wang's four-deletion comparison has a simple negative zero in each of the first \(\lfloor c_1\sqrt{n/\log n}\rfloor\) pivot gaps counted from zero and the first \(\lfloor c_2\log n\rfloor\) counted from the most negative root, with the middle gaps still open.

## Why it matters

Focused contribution

The proof establishes Ma and Wang's proposed zero comparison in ranges that grow with rank at both ends of the negative axis.
